Key Findings

1
Skills help most where they're needed most.
The Compact model shows the biggest gains (review-code +75%, research +133%), confirming that skills compensate for gaps in the model's built-in capabilities. The Frontier model shows near-zero improvement on most skills — it already does what the skills teach.
2
Office/media skills have the largest absolute impact.
make-sheets (+233% on Standard), make-slides (+150% on Frontier), make-docs (+67% on Advanced), and draw-charts (+67% on Advanced) are where skills add the most value. These are structured-output tasks where the skill's format requirements (formulas over baked values, assertion headlines, style-based headings) produce measurably better results.
3
review-security is the only skill that hurts.
Negative improvement on Compact (-50%) and Frontier (-20%). The thoroughness pressure inflates severities and suppresses correct "no findings" verdicts. The diff-attribution and clean-verdict rules added in the refinement commit address this.
4
simplify-code is unmeasurable.
On Compact the treatment run hallucinated its entire output (ratio -100%). On the other three models the baseline already simplifies perfectly (ratio 0%). No reliable signal.
5
[NEEDS] marking is the most transferable discipline.
The write-drafts improvement (26% avg) comes almost entirely from baselines inventing names/dates while treatment marks unknowns. This behavior transfers across every model tested.
